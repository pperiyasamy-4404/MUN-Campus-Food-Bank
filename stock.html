Food and stock

Volunteers and the coordinator now have a Stock page. Before each booking window opens, they count what's on the shelf and type the numbers in. When a donation of something new comes in, they add it. This page explains how it works and why.

Changed on 12 November: the number is optional. Most food now has no number. A volunteer taps In stock or Few left, and any number of students can book it. Only food worth sharing out exactly is counted, like 6 jars of peanut butter from one donation: the first 6 students get one, then it's out of stock.

Example: rice has no number. 20 students book it, and nothing is taken off. If a packer finds the shelf empty and taps Out of stock, rice becomes 0 and nobody else can book it until a volunteer taps In stock again. Peanut butter says 6: it works exactly as this page describes below.

Why: volunteers don't have time to count everything, and a count followed by a no-show could count the same food twice (what-could-go-wrong.html, problem 5). In the database, "no number" is varieties.stock = NULL, and "few left" is varieties.few_left. The rest of this page is about food with a number.

1What "stock" means

Food comes in three levels I1: a category (Breakfast), an item (Oats) and a variety (Quick oats). Stock is counted per variety, because that's what is on the shelf.

The number in the database, varieties.stock, is how many more students can book it. It isn't quite the number on the shelf:

When a student books, their box comes off stock straight away I5. But it stays on the shelf until a volunteer packs their bag. This is where a booked box is, for each booking status:

Booking statusWhere the food isOn the shelf?
bookedOn the shelf, waiting to be packedyes

cancelled, no_showBack on the shelf, and back in stock I6counted as normal

2Counting: what's on the shelf

The volunteer types the number they see. The server works out the rest:

stock = on the shelf − booked, not packed yet
    6 = 8            − 2

Why this way: the volunteer never has to think about bookings. And a count is right at any time, even while booking is open, so nobody has to remember when counting is "safe".

Why not "set stock to what I counted"? While booking is open, that would offer the 2 promised boxes a second time.

Why not a "+12 donated" button as well? With two ways of changing the number, it can drift away from what's really there. One number to type, the one on the shelf, can't drift. A donation is shelved, then counted.

Fewer on the shelf than were booked? Say 1 box is there, but 3 were booked. Stock can't go below 0 I4, so it's set to 0, and the page says: "Saved. 2 booked ones are missing from the shelf: those bags will need a substitute."

A count above 999 is refused, to catch a typo like 1000 for 10.

Counting while someone is packing (added 12 Nov)

The problem: Sam has a bag open and has put 1 jar of peanut butter in it, but hasn't pressed Packed. Alex counts the shelf. The jar is in Sam's bag, not on the shelf, and the server still counts it as "booked, not packed". So the count comes out 1 too low.

The count is still saved, but the answer now says who is packing a bag with that food:

Sam is packing a bag with this right now, so some may be in that bag, not on the shelf.
Count again after Sam presses Packed.

Why only a warning, and not an exact count? The server would need every Picked tick, and those stay on Sam's phone until Packed. A count that's a little low is the safe side: nobody books food that isn't there.

3A count and a booking at the same moment

Sam counts 5 tins and presses Save. At the same moment, a student books one. Without care:

    Sam (count)                       Student (booking)
1.                                    stock 3 → 2, the tin goes in their booking
2.  reads "booked, not packed": 0     (the booking isn't saved yet)
3.                                    saved
4.  stock = 5 − 0 = 5                 ← the booked tin is offered again

So the count locks the variety's row before reading anything (SELECT … FOR UPDATE). A lock means "nobody else may change this row until I'm done". The booking already holds that lock from step 1, so Sam's count waits until the booking is saved, and then sees it:

4.  stock = 5 − 1 = 4                 ← right

Tested for real: a test does exactly this on two database connections. With the lock it gets 4. With the lock taken out it gets 5, so it fails. The first version of this test passed either way, because Sam's count equalled the old stock, so nothing was overwritten. Starting the stock at 3 fixed that.

4What needs counting

Each variety remembers its last count: counted_at and counted_by_staff_id. A database rule keeps the two together, so a count always says who made it.

Food needs counting if it hasn't been counted since the last pickup ended. "Ended" means that day's no-show time, 6 pm, when every pickup, walk-in and no-show is settled. The page shows:

  • when the next booking window opens, like "Count the shelf before booking for Monday, November 9 opens, Sunday, November 8 at 5:00 p.m."
  • how many foods still need counting, with an amber edge on each one
  • a tick box: Only show food that needs counting

Hidden food never needs counting.

Changed on 12 November: the page now says needs checking, because most food has no number to count. Food needs checking if it was marked out of stock while packing, or marked out of stock and then found while packing (see packing.html, part 6), or if it has a number that was counted before the last pickup ended. Food with no number never needs counting. Checking it means tapping In stock, Few left or Out of stock, or counting it.

5Adding food

  • Something new (a donation of lentils): + Add food to book, with a category, the item ("Canned lentils"), its first variety ("Red lentils"), tags, and how many are on the shelf. An item can't be added on its own: with no varieties, students would see nothing. If there's only one kind, the variety can share the item's name.
  • A new kind of something already on the list (penne next to spaghetti): + Add a variety of Pasta, under the item.
  • Something the food bank stops getting: Edit → Hide. It's hidden, not deleted, because old bookings still point to it. Students stop seeing it at once.

Names

Names are unique ignoring capitals: "Oats" and "oats" are the same item. The database decides, with a unique index on the lowercase name. The server tries to save, and turns a refusal into words: "There's already an item called oats. Add a variety to it instead."

Why let the database decide, instead of checking first? If two volunteers add "Pasta" at the same moment, both checks would pass. The database rule can't be fooled that way.

A bug a test found: the save happens inside a savepoint, a small part of the transaction that can be undone on its own. For a rename, the new name was set before the savepoint opened. Opening a savepoint saves whatever is waiting first, so a refused name failed outside it, and the whole request broke. The name is now set inside the savepoint.

Rule I8 says volunteers or the coordinator update the food and the counts, and donations arrive while volunteers are there. On 6 Nov, categories, tags and names were for the coordinator only. You decided on 7 Nov that volunteers can do them too, since they're the ones at the shelf. The page asks people to check the list first, so there's only one of each. Bookings point to the row, not the name, so a fixed name shows everywhere.

7Extras

Extras like bread and eggs aren't booked online E1. On the stock page they show "10 left", not "can be booked". Students see them as "may be available" E2. Now an extra at 0 isn't shown to students at all, because it isn't available today.

11How to try it

Start both servers as usual, log in as your coordinator, and open Stock in the top bar. Your working database already has the new columns. Its sample food has never been counted, so every variety shows "Never counted".
