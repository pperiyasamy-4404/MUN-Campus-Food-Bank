Better backups

When a student books, they can choose up to 5 backups. A backup is food the volunteer puts in the bag if something the student chose has run out. The code calls these substitutes. This page explains what's wrong with how students choose backups today, what I want to change, and the three options (A, B, C).

Update, 14 November: idea 2 (two groups) was built in the dropdown first, then the dropdown was replaced. Backups are now chosen on the food cards themselves, with a "My food | Backups" switch: option 2 on backup-designs.html. This page is kept as a record of the first ideas.

How it works today

Two words first.

An item is a kind of food, like Cereal or Jam.

A variety is one version of that item, like Bran flakes or Corn flakes (both are Cereal), or Strawberry jam and Raspberry jam (both are Jam).

The rule today: a student can have only 1 of each item (1 cereal, 1 jam, and so on).

At the bottom of the food page there's a card called "Substitutes (if something runs out)". The student opens a plain dropdown and picks a name. Each pick goes into a numbered list.

Some things it already does well: it hides food the student already chose, food already on the backup list, and food that's run out.

How packing uses the backups

When a volunteer packs a bag and something is missing, the server goes down the backup list from the top. It skips a backup if it would give the student a second of the same item.

Example. Mia chose Bran flakes and Sunflower oil. Her backups: 1. Corn flakes, 2. White rice.

At packing, the Sunflower oil is gone:

Sunflower oil→ try 1. Corn flakes: skipped, she already has a cereal→ try 2. White rice ✓

But if the Bran flakes was the one that's gone, Corn flakes would be used, because then she has no cereal.

That rule is correct and fair, and I'm not changing it. The problem is that students can't see it.

The 3 problems

Problem 1: just names, no photos, no diet tags

Everywhere else on the food page, food is a card with a photo and tags like Vegetarian or Gluten-free. The backup dropdown only has names.

Example. Sam eats gluten-free and turned on the Gluten-free filter. Sam picks Pasta as a backup from the dropdown. The dropdown doesn't follow the filter, so Sam could get pasta Sam can't eat.

Why it matters: it's a health risk, not just looks. It's also the only part of the booking page that still looks old.

Problem 2: some backups can only fill one gap, and students can't tell

This is the Mia example above. Corn flakes looks like a normal backup, but it can only help if her Bran flakes runs out. If something else runs out, it's skipped.

Example. A student picks 5 backups, all other varieties of things they already chose (Corn flakes, Raspberry jam, …). Their own sunflower oil runs out, and none of the 5 can replace it. The bag is short and they have to choose at the counter, even though they set up 5 backups.

Problem 3: no help choosing

The dropdown is a long list in name order. It doesn't show which backups are most useful (ones that can replace anything), and it doesn't say when a backup matters most (when something they chose is almost gone).

The 5 ideas

Idea 1: a "Choose backups" pop-up with photo cards

The dropdown becomes a button. It opens a pop-up with the same food cards as the rest of the page. Tap a card to add it. It gets a number badge (1, 2, 3…). The chosen backups show as numbered chips that can be moved up or down or removed.

Why

Same look as the rest of the page, so nothing new to learn. Photos help people choose faster. It fixes problem 1.

Why a pop-up, and not the cards on the page itself

The page already has many cards. Putting backups there too would mix up "food I want" with "food if something runs out".

Why

It shows students the packing rule in plain words without them having to learn it. It fixes problem 2. Both groups are still allowed. A student who really wants Corn flakes if Bran flakes is gone can still pick it.

Why not hide the second group

Those backups are still useful for a cereal lover. Hiding them would take away a choice students have today.

Idea 3: the diet filter works in the pop-up too

If the student turned on Gluten-free or Vegetarian on the food page, the pop-up starts with the same filter on. They can turn it off if they want.

Idea 4: a hint on the Book bar when something is almost gone

If something the student chose shows Few left, the Book bar shows a short hint. It's only a hint and never stops them booking.

The Book bar

⚠ Sunflower oil is almost gone. Add a backup in case it runs out.
11 things · 0 backupsBook
Why

It tells students about backups right when they need them most. Fewer short bags means fewer people choosing at the counter, so lines move faster.

When it does not show

When the student already has at least one backup that can replace anything, or has chosen 5 already.

Idea 5 (later): "Suggest backups for me"

A button in the pop-up that fills in good backups by itself: food with plenty in stock, from the "Can replace anything" group, that matches their diet filter.

Why later

Ideas 1–4 are enough to fix all 3 problems. A "suggest" button also needs its own choices (Which food first? What about food nobody likes?), and it's better to see how students use 1–4 first.

Server and rules stay the same. Every idea changes only the booking page in the browser. The server already sends what the page needs: photos, diet tags, which item each food belongs to, and "Few left". The packing rule and the 5-backup limit don't change. The server tests stay as they are, and I add page tests.

What I would not do

A "Backup" button on every food card

Every card would have two buttons: "Add" and "Backup". The page we just cleaned up would get crowded again, and on a phone it's easy to tap the wrong one.

Changing the packing rule so a backup can give a second of the same item

That would break the "1 of each item" rule the food bank set, so it's not fair to everyone else. The fix is to show the rule, not change it.
