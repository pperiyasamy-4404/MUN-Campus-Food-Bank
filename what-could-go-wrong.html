What could still go wrong

All 9 steps are built and the walkthrough works from an empty start. This page lists what could still go wrong once real students and volunteers use it. I found these by walking through the system with you today and reading the code again. Each one has a small story, why it matters, the choices, and what I'd pick.

How to answer: reply with numbers and letters, like "1B, 3A, 5 wait, 8A". Say "explain 5 again" if a story isn't clear. Nothing on this page has been changed in the code yet.

The tags say how much each one matters:

  • Before real use must be sorted before real students use it.
  • Worth fixing won't stop anything, but people will run into it.
  • Your choice needs your answer. Ask the food bank only the food bank knows.

Before real students use it

1Nobody runs the 6 pm jobs Before real use

Decided on 11 November: whoever hosts the system runs the jobs every 15 minutes with the server's own scheduler. That's the hosting team's job, not the coordinator's, so no warning is built. The README will say how. The demo keeps running them by hand.

The jobs are the small script (python -m app.jobs) that does the work nobody clicks for. It makes next week's pickup days, marks bags nobody collected as no-shows, and deletes old data. On 10 November you chose to run it by hand, not on a timer. That's fine for a demo. My worry is real use.

A small story

You have exams and don't run the jobs from Thursday 12 to Thursday 26 November.

The jobs make pickup days only one week ahead. On Thursday 12 they made Thursday 19, but nobody makes Monday 23. On Sunday 22 at 5 pm, students open the app to book Monday and see no pickup days at all.

Also, Thursday 12's uncollected bag stays "packed" for two weeks. Its food is never offered to anyone else. And booking details older than 90 days aren't deleted, even though we promise students they will be.

Why it matters

Booking stops, food goes missing from the stock, and a privacy promise gets broken. All of it happens quietly, with no error anywhere.

Choices
  • A. Keep running it by hand, but show a red warning on the coordinator's page, like "The jobs last ran 3 days ago. Run them today." Small to build.
  • B. The computer runs it every 15 minutes by itself (Windows Task Scheduler, or the same thing on wherever it's hosted). Nobody has to remember.
  • C. Leave it as it is.

What I'd pick: A now, because it costs little and catches the problem. Then B once it runs somewhere real. B also makes question 5 almost go away.

2No backup of the data or the secret key Before real use

Decided on 11 November: it will run on a cloud, and the cloud's database backups are used. No backup script of our own. The secret key isn't in the database, so it goes in the cloud's secrets setting, kept apart from the database. The demo has only made-up data, so it needs no backup.

Nothing copies the database anywhere. There's also a secret key in backend/.env. The database never stores a student's ID number, only a scrambled version made with that key (a "hash"). When a volunteer types a card number, the system scrambles it the same way and looks for a match.

Three small stories

The disk breaks. In March the computer's disk dies. Every account, booking and weekly total is gone. 300 students have to queue at the counter again for new setup codes.

Only the key is lost. The database is fine, but .env was deleted. The system can't scramble "202312345" the same way any more, so no card matches any account. That's as bad as losing everything.

Both are stolen together. A thief has the database and the key. MUN numbers look like 2023xxxxx, so a computer can try every possible number quickly and find which scrambled code belongs to which number. Now the thief knows who uses the food bank.

Why it matters

We need both things backed up, but never in the same place. Then a single lost or stolen copy can't do the damage above.

Choices
  • A. I write a small backup script now. It copies the database every night into a folder you choose (later, somewhere off the computer). The key is kept separately, for example in the coordinator's password manager. We also try a restore once, because an untested backup often doesn't work.
  • B. Wait until we know where it will be hosted, because many hosts make backups for you.

What I'd pick: A. Even for the demo it teaches the restore steps, and a "how backups work" section looks good in the README.

3Only one coordinator Before real use

Built on 11 November (choice A): a coordinator presses Make coordinator next to a volunteer on the Staff page, or Make volunteer to go back. A coordinator can switch off or reset any other staff account, so a new coordinator can take over from one who leaves. Nobody can change their own account, so there's always at least one coordinator.

The system allows exactly one coordinator account (rule R3). Only the coordinator can close a day, change settings or add volunteers. Volunteers can't do any of these.

A small story

Thursday, 10 am. A snowstorm is coming and MUN closes at noon. The food bank should close today's pickup day so students don't walk through the storm. But Alex is sick in bed with their phone off. Sam and Jo are volunteers, so the Close button isn't on their pages. 12 students come in the storm, or don't come and become no-shows.

Later: Alex graduates and Jordan takes over. Jordan can't get a new account. They have to log in as "Alex", so every log line says "Alex" did it.

Choices
  • A. Allow several coordinators. A coordinator can make a volunteer a coordinator (and back again). Jordan gets their own account, and Alex's is switched off.
  • B. Keep one coordinator, but let volunteers close today's day too (only today, with a reason).
  • C. Leave it. In an emergency, someone with access to the server computer runs python -m app.first_coordinator Alex to get a new setup code for Alex's account.

What I'd pick: A. Real food banks usually have two or three people in charge, and handing over to a new coordinator becomes normal instead of a workaround.

4Who looks after it when you leave MUN Before real use Ask the food bank

Decided on 11 November: later. First the food bank has to accept the system, or it's worked on more. If it's handed over, everything will be explained clearly then. It's planned at that time, not now.

Any system that's really used needs someone to look after it: update it, fix it when something breaks, renew where it's hosted.

A small story

You graduate. Next September a volunteer says "the Hand over button gives an error". Nobody at the food bank knows Python or where the server is. The food bank goes back to paper, and students' accounts are lost.

Why it matters

A food bank is right to worry about this before they agree to use it. Having an answer ready also makes the project look more serious in an interview.

What I'd do
  • Write the README (already on your list), plus a one-page "If something breaks" guide: how to restart it, run the jobs, restore a backup, and give the coordinator a new code.
  • Ask the food bank or MUNSU (the students' union) who could own it later, for example a computer science student volunteer each year.
  • Where it's hosted is already on the "Before going live" list in CONSTRAINTS.md.

Problems in how the pieces fit together

These come from two parts of the system that each work correctly on their own, but can step on each other.

5Food counted twice after a no-show Worth fixing Your choice

Two things change the stock number in different ways:

  • A count on the Stock page sets the number: "there are 5 rice".
  • A no-show adds the bag's food back: "+1 rice".

If the count happens first and the no-show is marked afterwards, the same rice is counted twice.

A small story, with numbers

The same thing happens when a volunteer fixes a bag to "no-show" on the Fix page after the shelf was counted.

Choices
  • A. A rule for volunteers: "run the jobs before you count". Easy to forget.
  • B. A code fix: when a no-show puts food back, it skips food that was counted after that day's 6 pm, because the count already included it.
  • C. Run the jobs every 15 minutes (question 1, choice B). The gap shrinks from days to minutes, so it almost never happens.

What I'd pick: wait for the food bank's answer to question 13. Choice B is right only if uncollected bags are unpacked onto the shelf before anyone counts. If the bags sit in a back room for a few days, the count wouldn't include their food, and B would undercount instead. C helps either way.

Built on 12 November (a new choice, Prakash's idea): the stock number is optional. Volunteers don't have time to count every shelf, so most food is just In stock or Few left, and any volunteer can mark it. Food with no number takes nothing off and puts nothing back, so it can't be counted twice. Only food worth sharing out exactly, like 6 jars from one donation, gets a number, so it still can't be overbooked. What's left: food with a number, counted in the minutes before the jobs run. That's listed as a known issue, and question 13 now only matters for it.

6One wrong "Out of stock" tap spreads to every bag Worth fixing Your choice

When a volunteer marks something Out of stock and presses Packed, two things happen. Nobody can book it until someone counts it. And every other bag still to pack opens with that food already marked Out of stock, so nobody searches the shelf again (rule U7).

A small story

12:20 pm. Sam can't see the peanut butter, because it's behind the rice. Sam taps Out of stock and presses Packed. There are actually 10 jars.

12:30 pm. Jo packs the next 6 bags. In each one, peanut butter is already marked Out of stock, and since today's change those lines count as done. So Packed doesn't ask Jo to check. 6 students get a substitute (or nothing) instead of peanut butter, while 10 jars sit on the shelf.

Choices
  • A. A line that someone else marked Out of stock still needs one tap: "Still out of stock" or "Found it". The volunteer glances at the shelf once, so a wrong tap gets caught by the next bag.
    Bag M9G · 1. Put in the food
    Smooth peanut butter Sam marked this out of stock at 12:20 p.m. Have a quick look.Still out of stockFound it
  • B. Leave it. The Stock page already lists the food in red as "needs a count", and a count clears it.

What I'd pick: A. It costs one tap per bag, only for the food that ran out, and it fits the Picked idea: every line gets looked at.

Built on 12 November (choice A): the line opens as "Marked out of stock earlier", with Still out of stock and Found it. Packed waits until one of them is tapped. Found it on one bag doesn't clear the food for everyone: that's still done on the Stock page.

7Two volunteers pack the same bag Worth fixing Your choice

The packing queue shows "To pack" or "Packed", but not "someone is packing this right now".

A small story

Sam and Jo both open bag G6M on their phones at the same moment, and both start filling a bag. Jo presses Packed first. Sam presses Packed and the page says "already packed" (the database stops a double save). But there are now two full bags of food for G6M, and Sam has to put everything back.

Choices
  • A. When a volunteer opens a bag, the queue shows "Sam is packing". Anyone else who opens it sees a warning first.
    Packing queue · Thursday
    G6M 3:30 p.m. · Sam is packing (since 12:31)Open
    K3P 3:40 p.m. · To packOpen
  • B. Leave it. Volunteers split the bags among themselves, for example Sam takes 3:30–4:30 and Jo takes 4:30–5:30.

What I'd pick: wait for question 14. If only one person packs at a time, B is enough. If several pack together, A.

Built on 12 November (choice A): opening a bag to pack it puts "Sam is packing (since 12:31 p.m.)" on the queue. Anyone else who opens it sees a warning first, with Pack it anyway. Packed or Undo clears it. Built without waiting for question 14: it costs nothing when one person packs.

8Still logged in on a shared computer Before real use Your choice

A student stays logged in for 30 days (you chose this on 5 November). That's handy on their own phone, but not on a computer other people use.

A small story

Maya's phone is broken, so she books on a library computer. She closes the browser without pressing Log out. An hour later, another student opens the food bank site on the same computer and sees Maya's booking: her code, her food and her pickup time.

They can't take her bag, because the counter checks her MUN card. But they now know Maya uses the food bank, and they could press Cancel this booking.

Choices
  • A. A box on the log-in page: "This is a shared computer". If it's ticked, the login ends after 30 minutes without use, not 30 days.
    Student log in

    ID number · Password

    This is a shared computer (log me out after 30 minutes)

  • B. Everyone gets short logins. Safer, but students have to log in every time.
  • C. Leave it, and put a sentence on the page: "On a shared computer, always press Log out."

What I'd pick: A. Your own phone stays easy, and a shared computer stays safe. Banks and email sites work the same way. It also helps students without smartphones (already on the "Before going live" list), who will use shared computers more.

Decided on 12 November: left as it is. University computers log everyone out at the end of a session anyway. Listed under Known issues in CONSTRAINTS.md.

9Forgot the code, and the phone is dead Worth fixing Your choice

At the counter, a volunteer can find a bag only by its code (like G6M). The code is shown on the student's phone.

A small story

Ali comes at 3:30 with his MUN card, but his phone battery is dead and he doesn't remember the code. The volunteer has no way to find his bag, even though his card is right there. Ali goes home without food, or the volunteer guesses by looking at sticky notes.

Choices
  • A. The counter page gets a second way in: "No code? Type the MUN card number". It finds that student's bag for today. The card is checked anyway, so the volunteer learns nothing new.
  • B. Leave it. Tell students to write the code down.

What I'd pick: A. It's a small change, and a dead phone is common.

Built on 12 November (choice A): the counter page has No code? Use their MUN card. It finds today's bag for that card, and the hand-over box starts with the number.

10Older phones may show broken colours Worth fixing Your choice

The pages pick light or dark colours with a CSS feature called light-dark(). Only phone software from 2024 or later understands it: iPhones need iOS 17.5 or newer. An iPhone 8 or X can't update that far.

A small story

Sara uses an old iPhone 8. She opens the booking page. The colours don't load: buttons may look like plain text, and the green "booked" box may have no background. She isn't sure whether she pressed Book.

Honest note: I haven't tested this on an old phone, but I'm fairly sure about the cause. Students who need a food bank are more likely than most to have older phones.

Choices
  • A. Give each colour a plain light value first, then the light-dark() one. Old phones get light mode, which works. New phones are unchanged. It's a small change in one CSS file.
  • B. Leave it.

What I'd pick: A.

Built on 12 November, a little differently: a colour variable doesn't fall back to an earlier value, so "a plain value first" wouldn't have worked. Instead, light colours are set plainly and dark ones under a dark-mode media query, which every phone understands. Old phones now get dark mode too. Checked with axe in both modes, not on a real old iPhone.

11Holidays and term breaks Worth fixing Your choice

The system makes a pickup day for every Monday and Thursday. It doesn't know about holidays. The coordinator can close dates up to a year ahead (built in step 9), but only one date at a time, and only if they remember.

A small story

Say the food bank closes from 19 December to 5 January. Nobody closes those dates. On Sunday 20 December at 5 pm, students book Monday 21. On Monday they find the door locked. They were counting on that food over the break.

Choices
  • A. "Close several days": pick a first and a last date, and give one reason, like "Christmas break, back on 7 January".
  • B. Leave it. The coordinator closes each date, at the start of each term.

What I'd pick: A, and ask the food bank for their closure dates (they may already have a list).

Built on 12 November (choice A): Close dates on the Pickup days page takes a first date, a last date and one reason. Days already closed are left as they are, and the page says how many bookings it will cancel first. The food bank's closure dates are still worth asking for.

12No "Delete my account" button Worth fixing Your choice

A student account is deleted only after a year with no pickup (rule P13), and only when the jobs run. A student can't delete it themselves.

A small story

Maya graduates in April and doesn't want any record left. Right now she has to wait a year, and there's no button or form to ask for it.

Choices
  • A. A "Delete my account" button on the student's page. It asks first, and isn't allowed while they have a booking. The weekly totals stay, because they're only numbers, with no person in them.
  • B. A volunteer deletes it at the counter when asked.
  • C. Leave it: deleted automatically after a year.

What I'd pick: A. It's a strong privacy point, and a privacy review is likely to ask for it.

Decided on 12 November: left as it is. Accounts are deleted after a year with no pickup (P13). Listed under Known issues in CONSTRAINTS.md.

Questions only the food bank can answer

For the list in CONSTRAINTS.md under "To ask the Campus Food Bank".

13What happens to uncollected bags at 6 pm? Ask the food bank

Are they unpacked onto the shelf that evening, or kept bagged for a while in case the student comes back?

Why we ask

It decides how to fix question 5 (food counted twice). Since 12 November that's only possible for the few foods with a number.

14How do volunteers share out the packing? Ask the food bank

Does one person pack all the bags, or do several people pack at the same time? If several, how do they split the bags today: by time, by table, or by a paper list?

Why we ask

It decides whether we need "Sam is packing" in the queue (question 7). Built anyway on 12 November, so this is now only good to know.

15What numbers do you report, and to whom? Ask the food bank

After 90 days, booking details are deleted and only the weekly totals are kept. Weekly totals count pickups, not people.

A funder asks: "How many different students did you help from September to December?" The totals say 40 pickups each week, but that could be 40 students, or 10 students coming 4 times. Once the details are deleted, nobody can tell.

Why we ask

If they need it, we can keep a count of "different students this term" as a plain number, with no names or ID numbers. It's much easier to add now than to rebuild later.

16What if a student lost their MUN card? Ask the food bank

At the counter, the volunteer types the number from the card. The system can't tell whether the volunteer actually looked at a card, or just typed a number the student said.

Ali lost his card last week. He has a driver's licence and knows his student number. Should he get his bag? If "yes, any photo ID", someone who knows Ali's number and code, and looks a bit like him, could take his bag.

Why we ask

This is the food bank's rule to make. The system follows whatever they say, but volunteers need to know it.

Small things

  • "Staff log in" shows when staff are already logged in. The link at the bottom of every page is always there. It should hide when Sam is logged in.
  • Picked ticks are lost if the page reloads. Example: Jo's phone browser reloads the page after a call, and the ticks are gone. The Out of stock taps were already lost the same way before today. The fix: remember the ticks in the phone's browser until Packed.
  • Counting a food while a bag is half packed makes the number a little low. Example: Jo has put 1 rice in a bag but hasn't pressed Packed. Sam counts the shelf: that rice isn't on the shelf and it's still "booked, not packed", so it's taken off twice. Stock is 1 lower than it should be. It's on the safe side (it never offers food that isn't there), so a note for volunteers is enough: count before 12 pm or after packing.
  • No real screen reader test yet (already on the "Before going live" list).

Problems we already knew about

These are already in CONSTRAINTS.md under "Known issues" and "Before going live". They're listed here so this page has everything in one place.

  • The 5 pm rush: slots go to whoever is free when booking opens.
  • Password resets only at the counter.
  • Closures can't reach students: we have no email or phone number. This goes with the snowstorm story in question 3.
  • A volunteer could use a setup code first, since they see both the number and the code.
  • Internet down at the counter: no card or 28-day checks. A paper backup plan is still needed.
  • First visit in person: a new student can't book online until they've been to the counter.
  • A stranger guessing can delay a reset on a new phone by up to 15 minutes.
  • A fix to no-show can shorten the year rule (rare).
  • Students without smartphones, a privacy review, and where it's hosted.
