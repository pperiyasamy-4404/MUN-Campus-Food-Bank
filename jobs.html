Things that happen by themselves

Some things must happen even when nobody clicks anything. At 6 pm, a bag nobody collected becomes a no-show and its food goes back on the shelf. After 90 days, booking details are deleted and only totals are kept. An account with no pickup for a year is deleted. A small script does all of this every 15 minutes. And when a volunteer forgets to press Hand over, a new page lets them fix it.

1A bag's life after pickup day

2The jobs script

One small program, backend/app/jobs.py. It runs every 15 minutes, does whatever is due, and stops. On a laptop, Windows Task Scheduler can start it, through backend/run_jobs.cmd. On a real server, the server's own scheduler does.

  1. Make the pickup days for the next week.
  2. No-shows: bookings not picked up by 6 pm.
  3. Booking details older than 90 days: save their totals, then delete them.
  4. The activity log older than 90 days.
  5. Accounts with no pickup for a year. This comes after 3, so old details can't keep an account alive.
  6. Tidy up: logins, setup codes, wrong tries and holds that ran out.

What it prints each time, only numbers (P10):

Jobs, Monday, November 9 at 6:15 p.m.:
  2 no-shows. Their food is back on the shelf.

It looks at what's due now, not at when it last ran. Example: the laptop was off from 5 pm to 9 pm. The 9 pm run finds Monday's bags still "packed" after 6 pm, and makes them no-shows then. Nothing is missed, and a second run straight after finds nothing to do.

One run at a time. If a run is still going when the next one starts, the new one does nothing. A PostgreSQL "advisory lock" (a named lock that isn't on any row) makes sure.

Small transactions. Each no-show, each day's deletion and each account is saved on its own. So a student booking, or a volunteer counting the shelf, never waits long. If one piece fails, the others still happen, and the script ends with an error so the scheduler shows it.

Why not a timer inside the web server? It would only run while the server runs, and a restart would forget what was waiting. Why not a job queue like Celery? It needs another service running (a message broker). For a few small jobs every 15 minutes, a plain script is simpler. (The same choice as in tech-and-stack.html, part 12.)

3No-shows at 6 pm

A booking still "booked" or "packed" at the no-show time becomes a no-show B3. Its food goes back on the shelf, so others can book it I6. What goes back is what was really in the bag:

The bagWhat goes back on the stock count
Never packed. Booked: quick oats, sunflower oil. Substitute: lentils.Quick oats and sunflower oil. Not the lentils: substitutes are only taken from stock when they go in a bag U3.
Packed. The oil wasn't on the shelf, so lentils went in instead.Quick oats and lentils. Not the oil: it never went in the bag.
  • The no-show time saved is the day's (6 pm), even if the job runs at 9 pm.
  • A day the coordinator closed has no no-shows: nobody is a no-show on a day the food bank was closed. Closing a day will cancel its bookings (step 9).
  • Walk-ins stop at 5:30, but a booked bag waits for its student until 6 pm.

Hand over at 5:59, and the job at 6:00

A volunteer presses Hand over one second before 6 pm, just as the job starts. Both want the same bag. The job takes the same locks as the counter, in the same order: the student, then the booking, then the food. So it waits until the hand-over is saved, then reads the bag again, sees "picked up", and leaves it alone.

A test runs exactly this on two database connections. With the lock taken out, the job tried to mark a picked-up bag as a no-show, and the test failed.

4Fixing a pickup marked wrong

Volunteers are busy, and mistakes happen B6. A new page, Counter → Fix a pickup, lists the bags of pickup days that are over: who picked up, and who was a no-show.

The mistakeThe fixWhat changes
Sam handed over bag K3P at 5:50 but forgot to press Hand over. At 6 pm it became a no-show. They did take it → picked up The bag's food comes off the stock count again. The 28 days count from that pickup day. Recorded as picked up by whoever fixed it, at the time of the fix.
Bag M3T was marked picked up, but the student never took it. They didn't take it → no-show The bag's food goes back on the stock count, and so does anything given with it at the counter (the bread). The 28 days count from their last real pickup still on record, if any.
  • The page asks first, and every fix is logged with who made it. The log also keeps who pressed Hand over.
  • Fixes start once the day is over, at 6 pm. Before that, a bag that wasn't handed over isn't a no-show yet.
  • A bag that was never packed can't be fixed to picked up: "Bag T5J was never packed, so it can't have been handed over."
  • The page says when to fix by: "Fix mistakes before Wednesday, November 11 at 5:00 p.m., when booking opens for the next pickup day." After that, the stock count might already have been used for new bookings.
  • If the shelf count is already lower than what's in the bag, it stops at 0. The shelf can't have less than none.

A small known limit. Fixing a bag to "no-show" sets the 28 days from the student's last pickup still on record. If their only earlier pickup was more than 90 days ago, its record is already deleted. Then the year rule counts from when their account was made, which can be earlier. Example: account made 14 months ago, last pickup 5 months ago, today's pickup fixed to no-show. The no-show keeps the account for its 90 days, then it's deleted: 3 months after the fix, instead of 7. It needs that exact mix, so it should be very rare. The student would just get a new account at the counter next time.

5After 90 days: totals, then delete

We promised to delete booking details after the retention period (90 days with the sample settings), and keep only totals P7. So on Monday 8 February 2027, Monday 9 November 2026's details go. In one transaction:

  1. Work out the day's totals from its details.
  2. Add them to that week's row in weekly_totals and weekly_item_totals.
  3. Delete the day's bookings, with their food, substitutes and counter food, and its walk-ins with theirs.

Example. On Monday 9 November: one bag handed over (quick oats; lentils instead of the oil; bread at the counter), one no-show, one cancelled booking, and a walk-in who took 2 rice. The week of 9 November then holds:

weekly_totals       picked_up 1   walk_ins 1   no_shows 1   cancelled 1
weekly_item_totals  Oats 1   Lentils 1   Bread 1   Rice 2
No oil: it never went out. Nothing for the no-show or the cancelled booking.

Thursday's details go 3 days later, and its numbers are added to the same week.

Why save the totals just before deleting, and not every evening? Until the details are deleted, the totals can be worked out from them, and a fix (B6) changes them correctly by itself. Saving them every evening would mean changing saved totals after every fix. This way each day is counted exactly once: either it's still in the details, or it's in the totals, never both. The coordinator's page (step 9) will add the two together.

Why by week? The coordinator sees totals by week P12. Keeping less detail than that would be keeping more than we need.

Why by item, not variety? "12 oats" is what a food bank orders by. Varieties come and go.

What if two runs reach the same day? The day's row is locked first. The second run waits, then finds nothing left to count, and adds zeros.

The pickup day itself and its slots are kept: they say nothing about anyone. The activity log is deleted after the same 90 days, because it says who was served when (tables.html, choice G).

6Accounts with no pickup for a year

An account whose last pickup was more than a year ago is deleted P13, with its logins and setup codes. An account that never had a pickup counts from the day it was made, in Newfoundland time. Example, on Wednesday 10 November 2027: last pickup Monday 9 November 2026 is deleted. Last pickup Tuesday 10 November 2026 is kept.

Kept anyway, because someone is clearly using the account:

  • It has a booking or a walk-in still on record. Example: a student who never picked up, booking for next week.
  • It has a setup code that hasn't run out. Example: a volunteer gave them a new code five minutes ago.

Each account is locked and checked again just before it's deleted. If the student pressed Book at that moment, one waits for the other, and the new booking keeps the account.

The activity log keeps its lines about them until the log's own 90 days are up, but those lines no longer say who.

7Tidying up, and making days ahead

Setup codesOnce they've run outTrying one says the same as before: "doesn't match, or the code has run out".
Wrong triesOlder than 15 minutesOnly the last 15 minutes are counted P9.
HoldsOnce their pickup day is overKept until then, so a student whose 10 minutes ran out is still told "your time ran out".

Pickup days, a week ahead

Each run makes the pickup days from tomorrow to a week from today, on the weekdays with pickup hours (Mondays and Thursdays). A day already made is never touched, even a closed one. Today is never made: its booking would already have opened.

Why a week? (Your answer, 10 November. It was two weeks at first.) A made day keeps its own times, so a change to the settings reaches new days within a week. The coordinator can still close a date further ahead: the Pickup days page makes it first (step 9). Why not further ahead? Settings changes would take longer to reach students.

12How to try it

Run the jobs once, from the backend folder:

cd "D:\MUN Project\backend"
.venv\Scripts\python -m app.jobs

The first run on your working database made Thursday 19 and Monday 23 November. To see a no-show, a pickup day has to be over with a bag nobody collected. Ask me and I'll set one up on the test database, like the check above.

To run it every 15 minutes with Task Scheduler (question 1), the command would be:

schtasks /Create /TN "Campus Food Bank jobs" /SC MINUTE /MO 15 /TR "\"D:\MUN Project\backend\run_jobs.cmd\""
Each run adds a few lines to backend\jobs.log. Remove it again with:
schtasks /Delete /TN "Campus Food Bank jobs" /F
