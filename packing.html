Packing

On a pickup day, after booking closes, volunteers pack each student's bag. When something isn't on the shelf, the system says which of the student's substitutes goes in instead, and what to write on the sticky note. Other bags that need the same food get a warning. This page explains how.

1Queue, bag, Packed

Volunteers see the booking code (like G2T) and the food. Never anything about the student P4. A test checks that no answer from these addresses contains the student's hash or the word "student".

2When packing happens

Packing starts when booking closes K1. Before that, students can still cancel, so the list isn't final. Trying earlier gets:

Packing for Monday, November 9 starts at 12:00 p.m., when booking closes. Until then, students can still cancel.

Volunteers plan to finish by 3:30, but the system lets them go on until the no-show time. Why: only a packed bag can be picked up B2. If a bag was missed and the student is standing at the counter at 3:40, a volunteer can still pack it. At 6 pm, the day is over.

The queue shows the packing day: the first pickup day, not closed, whose no-show time hasn't passed. On a Monday that's Monday until 6 pm, then Thursday.

3The refill rule

This is rule U4, the one tech-and-stack.html said was the heart of this step:

Missing food is refilled from the substitute list, best first. A substitute is skipped if there's none of it, or if it would give the student a second of the same item. Refilling stops when the bag is back to its booked size.

It's one function, refill() in app/services/packing.py, and it doesn't touch the database. It only takes lists in and gives an answer back. That makes it the easiest code in the project to read, and the easiest to test. Here it is, shortened:

# How much of each item is in the bag now: booked, minus what's missing.
in_bag = Counter()
for food in booked:
    in_bag[food.item_id] += food.quantity - how_many_missing.get(food.variety_id, 0)
gaps = [one entry per missing thing, in the order the volunteer found them]

for substitute in substitutes, best first:
    if the volunteer looked and found none:     not_on_shelf
    elif every gap is filled already:           not_needed
    elif the stock says none left:              none_left
    elif in_bag[its item] >= max_per_item:      already_has_item
    else:                                       use   # and in_bag[its item] += 1

Example 1 from how-it-works.html: one thing missing

Booked

Quick oats, Gluten-free pasta, Sunflower oil (none on the shelf), Chunk chicken, Black beans

Substitutes

1. White rice: use, in stock and no rice in the bag

2. Vegetable oil: not needed

3. Red lentils: not needed

Sticky note: Sunflower oil → White rice

Example 2: substitutes that get skipped

Booked

Quick oats, Gluten-free pasta (none on the shelf), Sunflower oil, Chunk chicken, Black beans

Substitutes

1. Steel-cut oats: already has Oats (quick oats are in the bag)

2. White rice: none left

3. Red lentils: use

Sticky note: Gluten-free pasta → Red lentils

Both examples are tests, word for word: test_example_1_one_thing_is_missing and test_example_2_substitutes_that_get_skipped. So is the last line of example 2: if the quick oats had run out instead, steel-cut oats would go in, because missing food isn't in the bag.

Small choices inside the rule

  • "A second of the same item" means more than the per-item limit. With the sample limit of 1, that's exactly U4's words. If the coordinator ever allows 2 of each item, the rule still makes sense.
  • Each substitute fills one gap. Two things missing need two substitutes.
  • Gaps are filled in the order the volunteer found them. Any substitute can fill any gap, so this only decides which line of the note says what. Then a line doesn't change when another gap turns up.
  • A substitute with stock 0 is skipped, even if a few are on the shelf. Stock counts only what no other bag is waiting for. Those tins are promised to bags still to pack U3.
  • A hidden substitute is skipped. Hidden means the food bank stopped getting it.

Why a function with no database? The rule is the part most likely to be asked about ("what if two things are missing and one substitute is the same item?"). With no database, a test is three lines: food in, answer out. 11 tests check the rule on its own.

Why not work it out in the page, in TypeScript? Then the rule would exist twice, and the two copies could disagree. The server is the one place it lives.

4Nothing is saved until Packed

Tapping "Out of stock" or "Found it" only changes the page. After each tap, the page sends the server what the volunteer has found so far, and the server answers with the plan:

POST /api/packing/bags/12/plan
{"missing": [{"variety_id": 31}], "not_on_shelf": []}

← the answer: what happens to each substitute, and the note
{"substitutes": [{"rank": 1, "variety_id": 61, "outcome": "use", "replaces": "Sunflower oil"}, ...],
 "sticky_note": ["Sunflower oil → White rice"],
 "in_bag": 5, "booked": 5,
 "uses": [61]}

/plan saves nothing. So if a volunteer taps the wrong line, they tap Found it and the plan goes back. If they tap quickly, the page keeps only the answer for the latest taps.

Why not save each tap? "Out of stock" sets the food's stock to 0 (part 6). If that were saved at once, undoing it would need the stock number from before, and nobody knows it any more. Saving everything once, at Packed, means there's nothing to undo.

Picked: ticking off what's in the bag (added 11 Nov)

Each line also has a Picked button. The volunteer taps it as that food goes in the bag, and the line turns green: "In the bag". A substitute the plan puts in gets one too. Tapping it again unticks it.

Packed waits until every line is ticked: Picked, or Out of stock. Until then the bottom of the page says, for example, "2 lines still to tick, then press Packed." So a bag can't be marked Packed with something forgotten.

If the plan changes after a substitute was Picked (say the oil turned up after all, so the rice isn't needed), the rice line turns red: "Not needed now: take it out of the bag", with a Taken out button. Packed waits for that too.

Why only on the page? Picked helps the volunteer keep track while packing one bag. It changes nothing in the stock, so the server doesn't need it, and there's nothing to save or undo. Like Out of stock, it's gone if the page is reloaded, and the volunteer ticks again.

Why "Out of stock" and not "None on the shelf"? It's the shorter, everyday words volunteers already use. (The database still calls it not_on_shelf and ran_out_at: only the words on the page changed.)

5Pressing Packed: one transaction

Packed sends the same findings, plus uses: the substitutes the page told the volunteer to put in. pack() in app/services/packing.py then does this, all or nothing:

  1. Lock the booking. A second press of Packed waits here, then finds the bag packed: "Bag G2T is packed already, by Sam at 12:30 p.m." B2
  2. Check it can be packed: still "booked", the day isn't closed, and it's between 12 pm and 6 pm.
  3. Lock the food in the bag and its substitutes, lowest id first, and read the stock again.
  4. Work the plan out again. If it isn't what the page showed, refuse with 409. The page then asks for the plan again and shows what to put in now.
  5. Save: how much of each food was missing, which substitute replaced what, each substitute's stock minus one, "ran out" for anything nobody found, and the bag as packed, with who and when.

Why step 4: the last rice

Two volunteers pack two bags at the same time. Both bags are missing sunflower oil, and both list white rice first. One rice is left, so both pages say "put in rice".

Without the food lock:
  Mo reads rice: 1 left           Lee reads rice: 1 left
  Mo saves rice: 0 left           Lee saves rice: 0 left   ← both bags got the one rice

With the lock:
  Mo locks rice, reads 1, saves 0, lets go
  Lee was waiting. Now reads 0: the plan says vegetable oil, not rice.
  Packed is refused (409): "The substitutes changed while you packed..."

Lee's page shows the new plan, Lee swaps the rice for vegetable oil, and presses Packed again. A test runs exactly this on two database connections. With the lock taken out, it fails because both bags got the rice. A second test presses Packed twice at the same moment. With the booking lock taken out, it fails because the substitute's stock was taken twice.

The lock order is the same as booking and cancelling: the booking, then the food from the lowest id up. Packing never needs the slot. So packing can't deadlock with them.

6"Out of stock", and the flags

When Packed saves food that nobody found, it does two things to that food:

  • Its stock becomes 0. The shelf has none, so nobody else can book it until someone counts it.
  • It's marked as ran out (varieties.ran_out_at). Every other bag still to pack that has it gets a flag in the queue U7.

When a volunteer opens a flagged bag, that food is already marked "Out of stock", with "A volunteer marked this out of stock at 12:26 p.m. If there is some, tap Found it." So nobody searches the shelf for it again.

Counting the shelf clears the mark, because a count is what's really there. The stock page lists food that ran out as needing a count, in red: "Marked out of stock while packing, Monday, November 9 at 12:26 p.m. Count it to take the flag off other bags."

7The sticky note, and bags that go out short

Every swap goes on a sticky note on the bag U8, so the student sees what changed. The page shows the exact words, one line per gap:

Black beans → White rice
Sunflower oil → Vegetable oil

If the substitutes run out too, the gap stays a gap U6. The note says so, and the page warns the volunteer:

Sunflower oil → choose at the counter

The substitutes ran out too, so the bag is 1 short. At pickup, the student chooses something from the shelf instead.

The note is saved with the bag, so it can be shown again later: on the packed bag's page now, and at the counter in step 7.

8What the database remembers

No new tables. Four new columns, in migration c250b0a6f9de:

ColumnWhat it holdsDatabase rule
bookings.packed_by_staff_idWho pressed Packed.A packed bag always names who packed it, and only a packed bag does.
booking_items.missingHow many of this food weren't on the shelf. Usually 0 or 1.Between 0 and the number booked.
booking_substitutes.replaces_variety_idEmpty unless it went in the bag. Then: the booked food it replaced. The note comes from this.A foreign key on the pair (booking, food): it can only replace food booked in the same booking U5.
varieties.ran_out_atWhen a volunteer found none on the shelf. Cleared by the next count.—

Why save the swaps at all? Without them, the note would be lost the moment Packed was pressed. The counter in step 7 needs it ("this bag is one short"). And when a packed bag becomes a no-show (step 8), the food that goes back to the shelf is what was really in the bag, not what was booked.

Undo Packed

A packed bag's page has "Pressed Packed on the wrong bag? Undo Packed" at the bottom. It asks first, then POST /api/packing/bags/{id}/unpack does this, locking in the same order as Packed:

  • The bag goes back to "to pack", and shows in the queue again.
  • The substitutes that went in go back on the shelf, so their stock goes up by one again.
  • The booked food stays promised to this student, so its stock doesn't change.
  • "Ran out" marks stay. The shelf really had none, until someone counts it.
  • It's logged with who did it (undid_packed in the audit log), like other fixes B6.

It's refused once the bag has been picked up, after the no-show time, and on a bag that isn't packed. Rule B2 in CONSTRAINTS.md now has the move packed → booked.

12How to try it

Run the two servers as usual (see project-so-far.html, part 13). Then Staff log in → Packing.

To pack, a pickup day needs bookings, and its booking must have closed. In your working database, the sample days only open for booking at 5 pm the day before. So until the coordinator pages exist (step 9), ask me and I'll set up a day with a few bags, ready to pack.
