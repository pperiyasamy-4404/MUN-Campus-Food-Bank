Campus Food Bank · Step 7, the counter · 7 November 2026

The counter

This is where students actually get their food. Booked students say their code, a volunteer checks their MUN card, and hands over the packed bag. Students without a booking are served as walk-ins, from what's left on the shelf. Both count toward the 28 days, so nobody can walk in one week and book online the next.

1Two lines, one counter

The Counter page now has a small menu: Hand over a bag (/counter), Walk-in (/counter/walk-in), and Setup code (/counter/setup-code), which is the page from step 3.

2When the counter is open

On a pickup day, from when booking closes (12 pm) until the no-show time (6 pm). Handing over can't start earlier anyway, because bags are packed from 12 pm, and only a packed bag can be picked up B2. Otherwise the page says why:

The counter opens when booking closes, at 12:00 p.m.
Pickup for Monday, November 9 is over.
Today isn't a pickup day. The next one is Thursday, November 12.

Walk-ins are served only during pickup hours, 3:30 to 5:30 pm with the sample hours W3. (Your answer, 9 Nov.) They start the moment pickup starts and stop the moment it ends. A student with a booking who comes late, at 5:45, still gets their bag, because a booking waits until the no-show time. Outside those hours the Walk-in page says:

Walk-ins are served from 3:30 p.m. to 5:30 p.m., during pickup hours.
Walk-ins are over for today. They're served from 3:30 p.m. to 5:30 p.m., during pickup hours.

Each pickup day keeps its own pickup hours, like its booking and no-show times. They're copied from the weekly hours when the day is made. Why: if the coordinator changes the weekly hours, a day that's already made, with its slots, doesn't move. Why not read the weekly hours each time: the walk-in hours of a day already made would change, but its slots wouldn't.

The database also keeps a day's times in order: booking closes, then pickup starts, then pickup ends, then no-shows. Example: a day whose no-shows come at 5 pm but pickup ends at 5:30 is refused, because the last slots would be no-shows before their time came.

3Handing over a bag

  1. The student says their code, like K3P. The volunteer types it and presses Find the bag. Only today's bags are found.
  2. The page shows what's in the bag and the sticky note. If the bag isn't packed yet: "Bag K3P isn't packed yet. Pack it on the Packing page, then hand it over", with a link straight to it.
  3. If the bag is short, the student chooses from the shelf U6. They also say which extras they'd like E3.
  4. The volunteer types the number on the student's MUN card, and presses Hand over.

The server then does it all in one transaction: lock the student and the booking, check it's packed, check the card, take the chosen food and extras off stock, and mark it picked up, by this volunteer B3. The student's last pickup date becomes today, so their next pickup is 28 days later F1.

The card check

Booking online proves nothing. The card at the counter does P5. The server makes the keyed hash of the typed number and compares it with the one saved on the account:

typed 202300001 → keyed hash 3f9a…  ==  the booking's student 3f9a…   ← hand it over
typed 202300009 → keyed hash 71c2…  !=  3f9a…                         ← refused

"That card doesn't match bag N5D. Check the code and the card. Don't hand over the bag until they match."

The number is typed once here: a typo just doesn't match. The refusal never repeats the number P10, and the volunteer never sees the hash P4.

A short bag: choosing from the shelf

When packing couldn't fill a gap, the sticky note says "Sunflower oil → choose at the counter". At the counter, the page says "The bag is 1 short" and shows the shelf: only food nobody booked, with how many are left. The same limits as booking apply:

  • At most as many things as the gaps. They can choose fewer, or nothing.
  • The per-item limit counts what's already in the bag. A bag with quick oats shows "One Oats only" next to steel-cut oats.
  • Extras go under Extras, not here.

Once handed over, the bag's page shows "Also given at the counter" with what they chose and their extras.

4Walk-ins

The volunteer types the card number twice and presses Check the card. Twice, because there's no booking to compare with: a typo would record the walk-in under someone else's number and block them for 28 days. The check saves nothing, and has four possible answers:

WhoWhat the page saysRule
A card with no account yet"They can be served. This is their first visit: recording it makes an account for their card."W1
Picked up within 28 days, online or walk-in"Not yet. Their next pickup can be on Wednesday, November 25 or later." No override, as you decided.F1 W1
Booked for today"They booked for today: bag U3P, at 3:10 p.m. Hand over that bag instead."—
Booked for a later dayCan be served, but serving them cancels that booking. A checkbox must be ticked first.W4

Then the volunteer adds what they take from the shelf (up to 13 things, the per-item limit, food nobody booked) and any extras, and presses Record the walk-in. The answer says when their next pickup can be.

For W4, the cancelled booking's place and food go back for someone else, in the same transaction. Its cancel reason is walk_in.

5One of each extra, kept by the database

Rule E3: at most one of each extra item, like one bread, even if there's white and whole wheat. The code checks it, and tables.html promised the database would too. The trouble: a database rule only sees the row it's saving, and "is this an extra?" lives in the items table.

The trick is to copy the item and is_extra onto each row of counter_items, and then make the copies impossible to get wrong:

counter_items row:  variety 14 (White bread),  item 7 (Bread),  is_extra true

link (variety_id, item_id) → varieties (id, item_id)    -- variety 14 really is a kind of item 7
link (item_id, is_extra)   → items (id, is_extra)       -- item 7 really is an extra
UNIQUE (booking_id, item_id) WHERE is_extra             -- one Bread per pickup

Saving whole wheat bread as well is refused by uq_counter_items_one_extra_per_item_per_booking. Saving bread with is_extra false to dodge it is refused by the link. It's the same pattern the bookings table uses to keep a booking's day the same as its slot's day.

6The student's lock

A new "at the same moment" problem: a student is at the counter as a walk-in, while their phone presses Book for Thursday.

Without a student lock:
  Counter checks: last pickup long ago ✓      Phone checks: last pickup long ago ✓
  Counter saves the walk-in, today             Phone saves the booking, Thursday
  ← two pickups in four days

With it:
  Counter locks the student, checks, saves, lets go
  The phone was waiting. Now it sees today's pickup:
  "Your next pickup can be on Monday, December 7 or later."

So the lock order everywhere is now: the student, then the slot, then the hold or booking, then the food from the lowest id up. Book locks the student first, and so do both counter lines. A test runs exactly this on two connections, with different food so nothing else makes Book wait. With the lock taken out, the booking went through and the test failed. A second test has two walk-ins take the last tin at once. Without the food lock, both got it.

7What the database remembers

Two new tables, 21 in all, in migration 8ed315f3aef8:

TableWhat it holdsDatabase rules
walk_insOne student served without a booking: the day, when, and which volunteer (R4).One walk-in per student per day.
counter_itemsFood handed over that wasn't in a packed bag: extras, a choice for a gap, and everything a walk-in takes.Belongs to one booking or one walk-in. One of each extra item. An extra is one. The copied item and is_extra must be right.

Why not make walk-ins bookings? A booking has a slot and a code, and rules about both. A walk-in has neither, so half the booking rules would need switching off.

Why one table for extras, gap choices and walk-in food? They're the same columns. Three tables would repeat them three times.

9The tests

49 new tests, 358 in all, every one passing.

  • tests/test_counter.py: when the counter is open; handing over (right card, wrong card, not packed, twice, another day's code); a short bag and its limits; extras one of each, and only real extras; walk-ins (first visit, inside 28 days, twice in a day, booked today, W4 with and without confirming, limits, nothing left); booking online after a walk-in; and the two "same moment" tests.
  • tests/test_counter_api.py: the addresses the way the pages use them. Staff only, and no student hash in any answer P4.
  • tests/test_database_rules.py: the new rules, including trying to dodge "one bread" with a wrong copy.
  • tests/test_privacy.py: a second search for the card number P10. It goes through walk-ins, a typo and the wrong card at handover, and is then searched for in every answer, every log line (SQL included) and every database row.

The pages were also clicked through in Edge on a phone-sized screen against the test database: a short bag with a wrong card then the right one, a full bag, a bag not packed yet, a first-visit walk-in, a refusal, a student sent to their bag, and W4. That found one gap, now fixed: a picked-up bag didn't show what was given at the counter.
