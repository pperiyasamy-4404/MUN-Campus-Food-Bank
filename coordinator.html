2Closing a day

Snow on Thursday. The coordinator opens Pickup days, presses Close this day on Thursday, and types the reason students will see: "Snow storm". Before anything happens, the page says what it will do:

This cancels 2 bookings, 1 of them packed. Unpack those bags: their food goes
back on the stock count. Those students can book another day.

Then, in one transaction S7:

  1. Lock the day. If it's closed already, or over (past 6 pm), refuse.
  2. Lock all its slots. From here on, nobody can book it (part 3 says why).
  3. Cancel every booking still to come, packed or not, with the reason day_closed. Each one's place in its slot goes back.
  4. Put the food back on the stock count: what was really in each bag.
  5. Mark the day closed, with the reason, and log it.
The bagWhat goes back on the stock count
Ana's, not packed yet. Booked: quick oats, rice.Quick oats and rice.
Ben's, packed. No sunflower oil on the shelf, so lentils went in.Quick oats and lentils. Not the oil: the shelf had none, so it stays at 0.

It's the same rule as no-shows and walk-ins: packing.bag_contents(), written in step 6, says what's in a bag. A bag already handed over stays handed over, and a walk-in already served stays served.

Why cancel packed bags too? Nobody can collect them on a closed day. Cancelled, their food is counted again for the next day.

Why do those students book again freely? A cancellation never counts toward the 28 days F3. Ana can book Monday straight away.

Why must the reason be there? Students see it, and a closed day with no reason looks like a fault. It's up to 200 characters, and the page asks not to put anyone's name in it.

Why not let the job make them no-shows instead? A no-show can count toward the pickup gap, and nobody failed to show. The no-show job already skips closed days (step 8).

3Closing while someone presses Book

The coordinator presses Close at the same moment a student presses Book. Without care, this could happen: Book reads "Thursday is open", closing reads "these are the bookings", both save, and a booking is left on a closed day.

The fix is a lock (a row only one transaction can change at a time; the other waits). Book has always locked the slot before looking at the day. Closing now locks every slot of the day before looking at the bookings. So one of them always goes first:

Both cases are tests that run on two real database connections at once. With the slot lock taken out of closing, both tests fail: a booking stayed "booked" on the closed day. With it, both pass.

Why lock the slots and not the day? Book already locks the slot, in the order used everywhere: the student, then the slot, then the booking, then the food. Closing takes the same order (day, slots, bookings, food), so the two can wait for each other but never forever (a deadlock). Making Book also lock the day would add a lock to every booking, for something that happens a few times a year.

One small change to Book: after locking the slot, it now reads the day again from the database (_day_as_it_is_now), so it can't use an old copy from earlier in the same request.

4Opening a day again

A day closed by mistake can be opened again, if closing it didn't cancel any bookings, and the day isn't over.

Why not always? A student can't book the same day twice: a database rule (one booking per student per day) stops cancelling and booking again to change the food S6. So if Thursday is opened again after its 2 bookings were cancelled, those 2 students couldn't book Thursday any more, while everyone else could take their places. That's unfair, so the day stays closed, and the page says why:

"Closing Thursday, November 12 cancelled 2 bookings, so it stays closed: those students couldn't book it again."

Why not bring the bookings back? Their food may be gone by then, and their times taken. Asking first is safer: the Close button says how many bookings it will cancel before anything happens.

5Closing a date further ahead

Days are made two weeks ahead, but the Christmas break is known in November. Under the days, Close a later date takes any pickup date up to a year ahead, like Monday 21 December. The day is made now, from today's settings, and closed straight away. The jobs script never touches a day that's made, so when its time comes it's still closed.

A Tuesday is refused ("isn't a pickup day"), and so is today or a date in the past.

6Moving one day's booking times

Example: the website is down at 5 pm Sunday, when booking for Monday opens. The coordinator moves Monday's opening to 7 pm, so the rush starts when the site is back S9. Only Monday changes. The settings and every other day stay as they were. Bookings already made stay.

The checkWhat the coordinator reads
Booking opens before it closes.Booking has to open before it closes.
The new close time is still to come.The new close time has passed already. Choose a later one.
Booking closes by the time pickup starts, so bags can be packed K1.Booking has to close by the time pickup starts, Monday, November 16 at 3:30 p.m., so the bags can be packed
Only until booking closes.Booking for Monday, November 9 is over, and packing has started.

Why only until booking closes? After that, volunteers are packing. Opening booking again would let students cancel bags that are already packed. Cancelling was never built for packed bags, because it never happens otherwise.

The time boxes are St. John's time, wherever the computer is. The browser sends "2026-11-15T19:00" with no time zone, and the server reads it as St. John's T1.

7The settings

All of CONSTRAINTS.md's sample values, plus the weekly pickup hours, on one page in three groups:

  • For new pickup days: pickup days and hours, when booking opens and closes, the no-show time, slot length, students per slot. These are copied onto each day when it's made. Days already made keep their times, and the page lists them. To change one of those, move its booking times or close it.
  • Used straight away: the pickup gap, the time to choose food, the food limits, setup codes, wrong password tries, and whether a no-show counts.
  • Privacy: how long booking details are kept. A shorter time deletes more at the next jobs run, after saving totals.

Two kinds of check, so every mistake gets a sentence:

Each box on its own

A whole number in a range, like "Enter a whole number from 5 to 120." for slot length. The top of each range catches a slip, like 2800 for 28. The message appears under that box.

Boxes that have to fit together

The ruleExample message
Most of one item ≤ things per bookingThe most of one item can't be more than the item limit.
Details kept at least as long as the pickup gapBooking details have to be kept at least as long as the pickup gap. Otherwise a pickup could be deleted while it still counts.
Booking closes by the time pickup starts, every pickup weekdayMonday: booking closes at 4:00 p.m., after pickup starts at 3:30 p.m. It has to close first, so the bags can be packed.
No-shows after pickup ends, every pickup weekdayThursday: pickup ends at 6:30 p.m., after the no-show time, 6:00 p.m. No-shows have to come after pickup ends.
Pickup hours hold at least one slotMonday: the pickup hours are shorter than one 10-minute slot.
Booking opens at most 13 days beforeBooking can open at most 13 days before, because days are made two weeks ahead.
At least one pickup weekdayChoose at least one pickup day of the week. To stop for a while, close the days instead.

Why check these here, when the database has rules too? The database's rules are the safety net, but they only say "refused by ck_settings_…". These say what to change. And some can't be database rules at all: the hours are in one table and the closing time in another.

Why at most 13 days? A day is made two weeks ahead. If booking opened 20 days before, the day would be made after its booking had already opened.

Why save everything at once? The rules are between settings. Saving one box at a time, a change of two related boxes would be refused halfway.

8Weekly totals, and "fewer than 5"

One row per week, Monday to Sunday, newest first: bags picked up, walk-ins, no-shows, cancelled, and how much of each item went out.

Week ofBags picked upWalk-insNo-showsCancelledFood given out
Monday, November 97fewer than 5fewer than 5fewer than 5Oats 7, Rice fewer than 5
Monday, October 26125fewer than 5fewer than 5Oats 11, Pasta fewer than 5

Where a week's numbers come from. In step 8, a day's totals are saved into weekly_totals just before its details are deleted, in the same transaction. So every day is in exactly one place: the saved totals, or its details. The page adds the two together. A day counts once it's over, at its no-show time. Until its details are deleted, a fix on the Fix a pickup page changes its week's totals too.

Why "fewer than 5"? P12 A small number can point at someone. "2 walk-ins in the week of December 21" might be two people the coordinator knows were on campus.

Why 0 too? P12 says any number under 5. If 0 showed as 0, then "fewer than 5" would mean "1 to 4", which tells a little more.

Why on the server? The browser never receives the real small number: the answer itself says "fewer than 5". Hiding it in the page would still send it, and anyone could read it in the browser's developer tools.

The order of the food list is biggest first, and then the small ones A to Z. Sorting the small ones by their real number would give away which was 4 and which was 1.

9What students see

We have no way to contact students (a known issue). But a student whose day was closed sees this on their booking page, until that day is over:

The food bank closed Thursday, November 12, so your booking for that day was
cancelled. You can book another day.
Why: Snow storm

The list of pickup days already showed "Day closed" and the reason, since step 2.

13How to try it

The new migration is already applied to your working database. Start the two servers as usual (each in its own terminal):

cd "D:\MUN Project\backend"
.venv\Scripts\python -m uvicorn app.main:app --reload

cd "D:\MUN Project\frontend"
npm run dev

Log in as the coordinator at /staff/login and choose Coordinator in the top bar. If you haven't made a coordinator yet:

cd "D:\MUN Project\backend"
.venv\Scripts\python -m app.first_coordinator Alex
It prints a setup code. Use it at /staff/setup to choose a password.

Weekly totals will be empty until a pickup day with bookings is over. Closing a day and the settings work straight away.
