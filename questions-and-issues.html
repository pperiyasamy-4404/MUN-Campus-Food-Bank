Vital 1: Only a volunteer's click counts as "picked up", and mistakes can be fixed

Example: the student marks it

You said the student could also mark their items as picked up. But no-shows don't count toward the 28 days. So if Ben collects his bag and it isn't marked, he becomes a no-show at 6 pm and can book again on Thursday. A student who knows this just won't press the button.

Example: the internet goes down

Wi-Fi is down on Monday from 4 to 6 pm. Volunteers hand out 8 bags but can't mark them. At 6 pm all 8 become no-shows. The system adds their items back to stock even though they're gone, and all 8 students can book again on Thursday. Right now no-show is final, so nobody can fix it.

My suggestion

Only a volunteer's click counts. The student can't mark their own pickup.

Volunteers can fix a mistake afterwards: change a no-show to picked up, or back. Every fix is logged with who made it. It should be fixed before the next booking window opens, so the 28-day check and the stock count are right in time.

Vital 2: One held slot at a time, and check the rules before the hold

Example: many tabs

Sunday 5:00 pm. Ben opens 3 tabs and holds 3:30, 3:40 and 3:50. For 10 minutes, nobody else can have them, at the busiest moment of the week.

Example: not allowed to book anyway

Ana picked up last Thursday, so she can't book for Monday. She holds 4:00, spends 8 minutes choosing 13 items, presses Book, and only then sees "28 days haven't passed". Her hold blocked 4:00 for 8 minutes for nothing.

My suggestion

A student can hold only one slot at a time.

Check the rules when they pick a slot, before the hold: 28 days passed, no other upcoming booking, and they didn't cancel for this day. Then check again when they press Book, in case anything changed.

R3Round 3: the main issues left before we build

Your Round 2 answers are now in CONSTRAINTS.md, DECISIONS.md and how-it-works.html: one student per slot, no-show at 6 pm, no daily limit, a 10-minute hold, and no booking again the same day after cancelling.

I looked for anything that would change the database design or break the main rules. There are two real issues, plus a few quick yes-or-no questions.

Issue 1: Are walk-ins still recorded in the system?

You said we only control online bookings and don't care about walk-ins for now. Not limiting walk-ins is fine. But if walk-ins aren't recorded at all, two things break:

Example: the 28-day rule

Ben walks in on Monday and gets food. The system doesn't know. On Wednesday at 5 pm he books online for Thursday. He gets food twice in four days.

Example: the stock count

The system says 10 quick oats. Walk-ins take 6 on Monday, but nobody tells the system. On Wednesday at 5 pm, 8 students book quick oats. Only 4 are on the shelf, so 4 students get substitutes or have to choose at the counter.

My suggestion

Keep recording walk-ins, with no limit on how many. The volunteer types the card number and taps the items given. That's about a minute. It keeps the 28-day rule fair for everyone and the stock count right.

It also settles old issue 2: if a walk-in already has a booking, the system shows it, and the volunteer cancels the booking and serves them now.

If you'd rather not record walk-ins, then someone has to recount the stock before every 5 pm opening, and the 28-day rule only covers online bookings.

Issue 2: A forgotten "picked up" click

Example

Monday at 4:10 it's busy. A volunteer checks Ben's card and hands him his bag, but forgets to press "Picked up". At 6 pm the system marks Ben as a no-show. Three things go wrong:

  • His items are added back to the stock count, but they've left the building.
  • His 28 days never start.
  • He books again for Thursday and gets food twice in one week.
My suggestion

Make the card check and "picked up" one step. The volunteer types the card number. If it matches, the screen says "Give Ben his bag" and marks it picked up at the same moment. There's no separate button to forget.

Before 6 pm, a volunteer checks that the bags left on the shelf match the list of bookings not picked up. That's a 1-minute check that catches mistakes.

My suggestion: students per slot

One per 10-minute slot is a good start. Bags are already packed, so a pickup is quick: check the card, hand over the bag, add any extras. That's probably 2–3 minutes.

Keep the 10-minute slots and only change the number per slot. If all 12 slots go quickly every week and the volunteer at the online line is often waiting, raise it to 2 per slot (24 a day). It's one setting, no code.

Changing to 15-minute slots with 2 each gives fewer places: 8 slots × 2 = 16, while 12 slots × 2 = 24.

My suggestion: no-show or cancelled?

For the student, both give the same result: the items go back to stock and they can book again.

At 5:30 the system marks it as a no-show by itself, so volunteers don't have to remember. Volunteers put that bag's items back on the shelf. It doesn't count toward the 28 days, so the student can book again.

Why call it "no-show" and not "cancelled"? So the food bank can see how many slots are wasted.

Example: every Monday, 4 of the 12 slots are no-shows. That's a third of the slots wasted. With that number, the food bank can decide to make no-shows count toward the 28 days. That's already a setting.

New question A: Do we still need a daily limit, and places kept for walk-ins?

Example

Monday has 12 slots with one student each, so there are never more than 12 online bookings. The old rule (40 a day, 10 kept for walk-ins, online stops at 30) never does anything.

Walk-ins have their own line and only take what's left on the shelf after the bags are packed. So they can't take food from booked students.

But: if 45 walk-ins come on Monday and empty the shelf, students booking for Thursday on Wednesday at 5 pm see almost nothing in stock.

My suggestion

Drop the daily limit and the 10 places kept for walk-ins. Add one optional setting, "most walk-ins per day", left empty (no limit) unless the food bank wants one.

Bonus: the database gets simpler. A booking only has to lock its slot, not the day as well.

New question B: How long is the slot held while the student chooses items? (question 4, not answered yet)

Example

Sunday 5:00 pm, there are 12 slots and lots of students. Ana picks 3:40 and starts choosing. If the hold is 10 minutes and she gives up halfway, 3:40 is blocked for 10 minutes at the busiest time. If it's only 3 minutes, she may not finish choosing 13 items and 5 substitutes.

My suggestion

10 minutes, as a setting, with a countdown on screen: "3:40 is held for you: 7 minutes left".

New question C: Can a student cancel and then book again?

Example

Ana books at 6 pm on Sunday with oats. At 9 pm she wants pasta instead. She can't change items. But she can cancel (until 12 pm Monday) and book again, because cancellations don't count toward the 28 days.

She gives up her 3:40 slot and her items, and takes whatever slots and stock are left.

My suggestion

Allow it. It needs no extra rule, and losing her slot makes it a real choice, not an easy swap.

New question D: Can the coordinator move the booking times if the website goes down? (problem 3)

Example

The server is down on Sunday from 5:10 to 9 pm. Students lose the busiest hours of booking for Monday.

My suggestion

Yes. The coordinator can open booking later, or keep it open later, for that one day. It's a settings change, not code.

3The part you didn't get, explained with an example

Here is one student, Ana, booking for Monday with your order of steps: slot first, then items.

  1. Sunday 5:02 pm. Ana picks the Monday 3:40 slot. The system holds that place for her for 10 minutes, so nobody else can take it while she chooses.
  2. 5:02–5:08. She chooses her items (like sunflower oil) and up to 5 substitutes. Nothing is taken from stock yet.
  3. 5:08 pm. She presses Book. Now the system does three things: (a) turns her held place into a real booking, (b) takes her items off the stock count, for example sunflower oil 6 → 5, and (c) saves the booking with a booking code.

"What's a transaction?"

A transaction means the three things in step 3 happen together, or not at all.

Without it: the server crashes after (a) but before (b). Ana has a booking, but the sunflower oil is still counted as on the shelf. Another student books that "same" bottle. On Monday there's one bottle for two people.

With it: the crash also undoes (a). Nothing is saved, and Ana just presses Book again.

"Why reserve items only when they press Book?"

In step 2, Ana's sunflower oil isn't taken from stock yet. It's only taken in step 3.

If we took it while she was choosing: 6 bottles are left. 6 students add oil to their list, then close the app without booking. The oil looks gone, but nobody actually gets it.

The downside of our way: Ana chooses oil at 5:03, but someone else books the last bottle at 5:06. When Ana presses Book at 5:08, she sees "Sunflower oil just ran out, please choose something else". Nothing else about her booking is lost.

"Pressing Book twice still makes only one booking"

Ana's Wi-Fi is slow, so she taps Book twice. The system must make one booking and take one bottle of oil, not two of each.

"Issue 1: hold the slot for 10 minutes"

This is step 1. Your order of steps (slot first, then items) needs this hold.

Without the hold: 3:40 has one place left. Ana picks it at 5:02 and starts choosing. Ben books 3:40 at 5:05. At 5:08 Ana is told "3:40 is full" and has to start again.

With the hold: 3:40 is Ana's until 5:12. If she closes the app and never presses Book, the place becomes free again at 5:12.

4Your question: when should booking be open?

On 27 Oct we decided booking opens at 10 am two days before and closes 3 hours before the slot. That no longer fits, because:

  • items can't be changed after booking, and volunteers pack before 3 pm on the pickup day, so booking has to close before packing starts;
  • you want evening times, so students with classes can book.

Here are your idea and my suggestion, shown for Monday. Thursday works the same way: Wednesday evening → Thursday.

My suggestion: Option B. Open at 5 pm the day before, like your idea, but stay open until packing starts the next morning.

  • It costs nothing in stock accuracy. There are no pickups between Sunday 5 pm and Monday morning, so the stock doesn't change. Staying open longer doesn't make the counts any less accurate.
  • It helps more students. Someone working or in a lab from 5 to 7 pm can still book at 9 pm or 7 am.
  • Volunteers still get every order before they start packing.

What it doesn't fix: if more students want food than there are places, the places will still be gone a few minutes after 5 pm (problem 1 below).

Allow cancel-and-book-again while booking is open. She gives up her slot and her items and takes whatever is left, so nobody uses it as an easy way to swap. It also needs no extra rule.

6Problems that could happen

These are the places where I think the system could fail or treat someone unfairly. Each one says whether it's Handled, a Known issue we accept for now, or Needs a decision.

Problem 1: The 5 pm rush Known issue

150 students want food for Monday, and there are 30 online places. At 5:00 pm on Sunday everyone opens the app. By 5:04 every place is gone. A student who has a lab every Sunday at 5 pm never gets a place.

Why: first come, first served always favours whoever is free at opening time.

Later ideas: a random draw among everyone who asks during the evening, instead of first come, first served. First, ask the food bank how many students come today. If it's fewer than 30, the rush never happens.

Problem 2: Forgetting the password on booking night Known issue

Ben forgets his password on Sunday at 5 pm. Passwords are only reset at the counter, and the food bank is closed on Sunday. He can't book for Monday.

What he can do: walk in on Monday, if a walk-in place is left, and get a new setup code at the counter at the same time.

Later: a MUN-email reset would fix this, if MUN ever gives access.

Problem 3: The website is down while booking is open Needs a decision

The server crashes on Sunday at 5:10 pm and comes back at 9 pm. With Option A (5–7 pm only), nobody can book for Monday at all. With Option B, students lose a few hours but can still book later that night.

Idea: let the coordinator change that day's closing time if something goes wrong. It's just a setting.

Problem 4: The internet is down at the counter Known issue

Monday 3:45, the Wi-Fi drops. Volunteers can't check MUN cards against bookings, or the 28-day rule for walk-ins.

Idea: ask the food bank what they do today. A printed list of the day's booking codes could be a backup, as long as it has no ID numbers on it.

Problem 5: A snow day after packing Known issue

Monday at 1 pm, MUN closes for a storm. 30 bags are already packed. The coordinator cancels Monday, and the system puts every item back into the stock count.

Two things to watch: volunteers must really unpack the bags, or the stock on screen won't match the shelf. And those 30 students can book Thursday, but Thursday still has only 30 online places, so many of them won't get one.

Later idea: give students from a closed day first chance at the next day.

Problem 6: The stock count is wrong Handled

The system says 5 sunflower oils, but the shelf has 3. Five students booked oil. Three get oil. The other two get their first substitute that's in stock, with a sticky note on the bag. If all their substitutes are gone too, the volunteer asks them at the counter.

The volunteer marks sunflower oil as out of stock, which flags every other booking with oil (rule U7). The coordinator then fixes the count.

Problem 7: A new student can't book until they've been to the counter Known issue

A student hears about the food bank on Tuesday. They can't book online without an account, and accounts are only made at the counter. The next counter time is Thursday 3:30–5:30.

What happens: on Thursday they get an account and can take food as a walk-in at the same visit, so they don't lose a week. We accepted this cost on 27 Oct. It's here because the counter is now only open 4 hours a week.

Problem 8: Unused walk-in places (issue 12) Mostly solved by the new times

Only 4 walk-ins come on Monday, so 6 of the 10 walk-in places are unused. Booking closed that morning, so those places can't be offered online.

But the other way round works on its own: if only 20 people booked online, walk-ins can use the empty online places too, because the day limit is 40 in total.

So the only waste is unused walk-in places, and that's fine.
