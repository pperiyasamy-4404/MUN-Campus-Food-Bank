Booking

Students can now book. They choose a time, which is kept for them for 10 minutes. They choose their food and up to 5 substitutes, and press Book. They get a code like K3P to say at the counter, and they can cancel until booking closes. This is the heart of the system, so this page goes slowly.

Updated 13 November: the food bank's real hours are Monday and Thursday, 3:30 to 7:30 pm, with no-shows at 8 pm. The times on this page say so.

1Three stages, one page

Which stage a student sees comes from the server: GET /api/my-booking says whether they have a booking, a hold, or neither. So a reload, or opening the page on another device, never loses their place.

2Who may book which day

One function, why_not in app/services/booking.py, answers "why can't this student book this day right now?" in words, or says nothing if they can. It runs before the hold, and again when they press Book, in case something changed while they chose S8.

The pickup gap, in calendar days

The last pickup is the latest of: the date on the student's account (which will also cover walk-ins, in step 7), a picked-up booking, and a no-show if the setting says no-shows count. Cancellations never count F3. Then it adds 30 days:

last pickup  Monday 12 October  + 30 days  =  Monday 9 November   ← allowed
last pickup  Tuesday 13 October + 30 days  =  Tuesday 10 November  ← Monday 9 is one day early

Calendar days, not hours: a pickup at 5 pm and one at 3:30 pm four weeks later are 30 days apart, as rule F1's own example says.

3Holding a time

A hold keeps a time for one student for 10 minutes while they choose food S8. It's a row in the holds table: the student, the slot, and held_until.

  • One per student. The student is the table's primary key, so a second hold replaces the first. Nobody can block several times at 5 pm by opening tabs.
  • Holding the same time again adds no time. Otherwise a student could keep a time forever.
  • Others see it as taken. "Places left" on every page counts bookings and holds that haven't run out.
  • Running out needs no clean-up. A hold past held_until simply counts as free. Nothing has to delete it on the dot.

Before giving a hold, the server locks the slot's row and counts: bookings + other students' live holds. If that's already the capacity, the answer is "Someone else just took that time. Choose another one."

Why a countdown from the server? The answer includes seconds_left, counted by the server. A phone whose clock is 3 minutes fast would otherwise show the wrong time left. The server decides anyway: Book after the time is up is refused.

4Pressing Book: one transaction

A transaction is a group of changes that are saved all together, or not at all. Booking is one, exactly as planned on 3 November in tables.html:

  1. Lock the slot, then read the student's hold again. Still theirs? Not run out?
  2. Check the rules again with why_not: the day, the gap, other bookings.
  3. Check the food: the limits and the substitutes (section 6).
  4. Take a place: the slot's booked_count goes up by 1. The database refuses going over capacity S2.
  5. Take the food off stock, lowest id first, each row locked. Not enough left? "Sorry, Chunk chicken has just run out." I4
  6. Save it all: the booking with a new code, its food, its ranked substitutes. Delete the hold.

If step 5 refuses, the route never commits, so steps 1 to 4 are undone too. The student keeps their hold and their list on the page, and can swap the food that ran out.

The booking code

Codes look like K3P: a letter, a digit, a letter. There's no I, L or O, and no 0 or 1, so nothing gets misheard at the counter. The pattern can't spell a word. That gives 4,232 codes per day. The server picks one nobody has that day, and the database's unique rule is the safety net P4.

5Locks, and the order they're taken in

Three things can go wrong when people act at the same moment. Each one is a test that runs two real database connections at once:

Two at onceWithout a lockWith the lock
Ana and Ben hold the last placeBoth get a holdBen waits, then sees Ana's hold: "Someone else just took that time."
Ana and Ben book the last tin (different times)Both get the tin, stock 0, two bookingsBen waits, then sees 0: "Sorry, Last tin has just run out."
Ana presses Book twice (a double click)—The second waits, then finds her booking: "You're booked already. Your code is K3P." B4

Do the tests really catch it? The first two were run with their lock taken out, and both failed: two holds on the last place, and two bookings of the last tin. With the locks, they pass.

Every lock is taken in the same order: the slot, then the student's hold or booking, then the food from the lowest id up. Booking, cancelling and holding all follow it.

Why the order matters: if one booking locked oats then chicken, and another locked chicken then oats, each could end up waiting for the row the other has, forever. That's a deadlock. If everyone locks in the same order, it can't happen.

6The food rules

These need the settings or other tables, so the code checks them, on the page (to say no straight away) and on the server (which really decides):

RuleWhat the student reads
At least one thing"Choose at least one thing."
Up to 13 things in total I3"You can choose up to 13 things. Your list has 14."
One of each item, counting all its varieties I3On the page, the other oats say "One Oats only". From the server: "You can choose 1 Oats at most, counting all its kinds together."
Extras aren't booked E1"Bread is an extra: ask for it at pickup instead."
Hidden food can't be booked"White rice isn't on the list any more. Choose something else."
Up to 5 substitutes, not extras, not already on the list, each once U2"A substitute can't be something already on your list."

Substitutes are ranked: if something is gone at packing, the volunteer uses them in order (step 6). They aren't taken off stock U3. The page only offers substitutes that are in stock, because one at 0 couldn't fill a gap.

7Cancelling

Until booking closes (12 pm on the pickup day), a student can cancel S6. The place goes back in the slot and the food back on stock I6, so someone else can have them straight away. Locks follow the same order as booking: the slot, the booking, then the food.

After cancelling, that day can't be booked again, by a database rule (one booking per student per day, of any status). Otherwise cancelling and booking again would be a way to change the food I7. A later day is fine, because cancellations don't count toward the gap.

8The answers the page gets

AddressWhat it does

DELETE /api/holdsLet go of it ("Choose a different time")
POST /api/bookings{"items": [{"variety_id": 11}], "substitutes": [31, 25]}. Book
POST /api/my-booking/cancelCancel

None of these takes a booking id: they always mean the logged-in student's own. Nobody can see or cancel someone else's booking by changing a number.

When the server says no, the status number tells the page what to do, so rewording a message can never break it:
