Ideas to make the website even better

The website works and looks good now. This page is a list of ideas to make it easier, faster and friendlier for students, volunteers and the coordinator. Each idea says what it is, a tiny example, why it helps, and how much work it is. At the end there are tricks to find more ideas yourself, and a few ideas I would not do.

For students

S1Your booking code works with no internet

Small workBig help
What it is

When a student books, the phone keeps a copy of their code, day and time. If there's no signal at the counter, the booking page still shows the code.

Tiny example

Priya walks into the basement, her phone says "No service". She opens the website from her home screen and still sees Q3J · Monday 3:30 pm, with a small note "Saved on this phone".

Why it helps

The code is the one thing a student needs at the counter. Losing it means a longer line while the volunteer searches by MUN card.

Watch out

Keep only the code, day and time on the phone, never the ID number. Clear it when they log out or the day is over.

S2"Add to my calendar" after booking

Small workBig help
What it is

A button on "You're booked!" that downloads a small calendar file. The phone's own calendar adds the pickup with a reminder an hour before.

Tiny example

Tap Add to my calendar → the phone shows "Campus Food Bank pickup, Mon 16 Nov, 3:30 pm, code Q3J. Bring your MUN card." → reminder at 2:30 pm.

Why it helps

Students are busy and forget. Every no-show is a bag packed for nobody. A reminder is the cheapest way to cut no-shows.

Why not email or text reminders

The site keeps no email or phone number, on purpose (privacy). A calendar file needs nothing new to be stored.

S3Change your food without cancelling

Big workBig help
What it is

Until booking closes, a student can open their booking and swap food or change the time, instead of cancelling.

Tiny example

Ali booked oats, then sees eggs came in. Today he must cancel, and then he can't book that day again (rule S6). With this, he taps Change my food, swaps oats for cereal, done.

Why it helps

People change their minds. Today the only way out is a cancel that locks them out of that day, which feels harsh.

Watch out

It touches the stock rules (food goes back, new food comes off) and needs careful tests, like booking did. That's why it's big.

S4"Book the same as last time"

Medium work
What it is

On the food page, one button fills in last time's food and substitutes (only what's still in stock). The student can still change anything.

Tiny example

Most students take the same 10 things every month. Instead of 10 taps: 1 tap, check, book.

Why it helps

Faster booking inside the 10-minute hold, so fewer holds run out.

Watch out

It only works while the last booking's details are still kept (P7 deletes them after a while). If they're gone, the button just doesn't show.

S5Keep the food list if the 10 minutes run out

Small work
What it is

If the hold runs out, the food they chose is remembered on the phone. When they pick a new time, it's filled back in.

Tiny example

Sara chose 9 things, her friend called, the 10 minutes ran out. Today: start again from zero. With this: "We kept your list" and her 9 things are back.

Why it helps

Losing work is the most annoying thing on any website. This removes it.

S6A tap when booking opens (notifications)

Medium work
What it is

Students who add the site to their home screen can turn on a notification: "Booking for Monday is open now."

Why it helps

Popular times go fast. A notice at 5 pm is fairer than students refreshing the page all afternoon.

Watch out

Only if the student turns it on. Never more than one message per pickup day, or people turn it off.

S7Remember the diet filters

Small work

If a student ticks Vegetarian once, keep it ticked next time on that phone. A vegetarian student shouldn't have to tick it every visit. Kept only on their own phone, nothing on the server.

S8A big "show at the counter" screen

Small work

A button on the booking page that fills the whole screen with the code in huge letters, on a white background. The student turns their phone to the volunteer, and the volunteer reads it from a metre away. No squinting, no "can you say that again".

S9Other languages

Big workBig help
What it is

A language button: English, French, and the languages MUN's international students use most (ask the food bank which).

Why it helps

Many students who need the food bank are new to Canada. Reading food names and rules in your own language makes it far less stressful.

Watch out

Every sentence on the site has to move into a translation file, and every new sentence needs translating. Do it once the words stop changing.

For volunteers (packing, counter, walk-ins)

V1One total pick list for packing

Small workBig help
What it is

At the top of the Packing page: everything the day's bags need, added up, in shelf order.

Tiny example

Monday, 18 bags:

Cereal: 12 (Bran flakes 7, Corn flakes 5) · Jam: 9 · Oats: 6 · Canned tuna: 4 ...

Why it helps

A volunteer brings all 12 cereals to the table in one trip, then fills the bags. It's how warehouses pack, and it's much faster than walking to the shelf for every bag.

V2"Coming next" list at the counter

Small workBig help
What it is

On Hand over a bag: a small list of the bags due in the next 30 minutes, with their codes and whether they're packed.

Tiny example

4:00 pm Q3J ✓ packed · 4:10 pm R7S ✓ packed · 4:20 pm K2M not packed yet

Why it helps

The volunteer puts the next bags near the counter before students arrive, and spots an unpacked bag before the student is standing there waiting.

Watch out

Codes and times only. No names or ID numbers (P4).

V3Print bag labels

Small work

A Print labels button on the Packing page that prints each bag's code and pickup time in large letters, plus the sticky-note lines (like "Oats → Rice"). Volunteers tape one on each bag. No more hand-writing codes, and no bad handwriting at the counter.

V4Undo for 10 seconds after "Handed over"

Medium work

After handing over a bag, show a message with Undo for 10 seconds. Busy counters make mistakes (wrong bag, a student who walks away). Today the fix is the separate Fix a pickup page, which is slower and feels serious. Undo is quick and calm.

V5The keyboard does everything at the counter

Small work

The cursor is already in the code box when the page opens. Enter finds the bag, then jumps to the MUN card box. Enter again hands it over. A volunteer serving 40 students saves hundreds of mouse moves, and the line moves faster.

V6Scan instead of type

Medium work
What it is

If MUN cards have a barcode, a cheap USB scanner (about $25) can type the ID number into the box. The scanner acts like a keyboard, so the website barely needs to change.

Why it helps

No typos, and no typing the number twice on Walk-in and Setup code.

Watch out

Check first that the card's barcode really is the ID number. Don't add phone-camera scanning of booking codes: a 3-letter code is already quicker to say than to scan.

V7Packing updates live

Medium work

Today the Packing page asks the server again every 30 seconds. With live updates, when one volunteer marks Tuna "ran out", every other volunteer sees it at once, not up to 30 seconds later. Nice, but 30 seconds is usually fine, so this can wait.

For the coordinator

C1A "running low" list

Small workBig help
What it is

On Stock or the Coordinator page: food that is low or out, with how many were booked last week.

Tiny example

⚠ Canned tuna: 0 left, 9 booked last week · ⚠ Chickpeas: 1 left, 6 booked last week

Why it helps

It becomes the shopping and donation-request list. The coordinator knows what to ask donors for without walking the shelves.

C2Charts on the weekly totals

Medium work

The totals are a table of numbers today. A simple line chart ("bags per week this term") and a bar chart ("most booked food") make reports to MUN or donors easy, and show trends like the rush before exams. Keep the privacy rule: small numbers still show as "fewer than 5" (P12).

C3Download the totals as a spreadsheet

Small work

A Download (CSV) button that opens in Excel. Coordinators often have to send numbers to someone, and copying from a web page is slow and error-prone. Same privacy rule as the charts.

C4A notice banner for students

Small work

A box on the Coordinator page: type "Extra eggs this week!" or "Closed 21 Dec to 6 Jan", and it shows at the top of the home page until a chosen date. The coordinator can tell every student something without a developer.

For everyone

E1A short "How it works" page

Small workBig help

Five questions with short answers, linked from the home page:

  • How do I get an account? (Go to the counter with your MUN card.)
  • How often can I get food? (Once every 30 days.)
  • What if I can't come? (Cancel before booking closes.)
  • What if something runs out? (Your substitutes, then the shelf.)
  • Is my information private? (What's kept, and for how long.)

It saves volunteers from answering the same questions all evening, and the privacy answer builds trust.

E2A 3-step welcome for first-time students

Small work

The very first time a student logs in: three small cards, "1 Pick a time → 2 Choose food → 3 Say your code at the counter", and a Got it button. Shown once. People learn faster from three pictures than from a page of rules.

E3Every error has a "Try again" button

Small work

Today some failures say "Could not get the food list. Is the server running?" That's a developer's sentence. Better: "We couldn't load the food. Check your internet and try again." with a Try again button. Tell people what happened and what to do, in their words.

E4Lighter photos, a faster site

Small work

Save food photos in a modern format (WebP) at the size they're shown. Photos are usually most of a page's weight. On a cheap phone with weak signal, the food page could load in about half the time. Test it once on the slowest phone you can find.

E5Try it with only a keyboard and a screen reader

Small work

The automatic checker (axe) says the pages are clean, but it can't catch everything. Spend 20 minutes booking with only the Tab and Enter keys, then with NVDA, the free Windows screen reader. If you get stuck, a blind or low-vision student would too.

E6Make every page follow the same pattern

Medium work

Log in, Set up and Setup code are now neat centred cards. Walk-in, Fix a pickup and Settings are still plain forms. Pick one look for "a form you fill in" and use it everywhere: same widths, same button place, same spacing. When things look the same, people trust them and learn them once.

Tricks to find more ideas yourself

The best ideas come from watching real people, not from guessing. These are simple and free.

T1Watch 5 people book

1 hourBig help

Ask 5 friends (or real students) to book a pickup while you watch. Say only: "Please book a pickup for Monday. Say out loud what you're thinking." Don't help them, even when they get stuck. Write down every pause, every wrong tap, every "hmm".

Why 5: after about 5 people you keep seeing the same problems again. Five people find most of the big ones.

T2The 5-second test

10 minutes

Show someone a page for 5 seconds, then hide it. Ask: "What was that page for? What would you click first?" If they can't say, the page's main point isn't clear enough.

T3Ask volunteers three questions after a shift

5 minutes
  • What was slow tonight?
  • What did students ask you most?
  • What did you do on paper instead of the website?

The third question is gold: anything done on paper is something the website is missing.

T4Count where people give up

Medium work

Count things, not people. Example: "This week, 23 holds ran out before booking." If that number is high, the food page is too slow or confusing. Other good counts: cancellations, no-shows, setup codes never used. Numbers only, no names, so privacy stays safe.

T5Read every page out loud

30 minutes

If a sentence sounds strange when you say it, it reads strange too. Also check that each page has one main button. When everything shouts, nothing does.

T6Look at the error log every week

5 minutes

The server already writes its errors to errors.log. Five minutes a week reading it finds bugs before students report them, or before they give up quietly and never tell you.

Ideas I would not do (and why)

Dark mode

It's popular, but MUN's brand colours were chosen for light backgrounds, and you decided on light only (12 Nov). Claret on dark grey is hard to read. Only revisit if students ask for it.

A chatbot

A good "How it works" page (E1) answers the same questions better, costs nothing to run, and never gives a wrong answer about the rules.

Email or text reminders

They'd need students' email addresses or phone numbers, which the site deliberately doesn't keep. The calendar button (S2) and notifications (S6) give the same help without any new personal data.

Points, badges or "streaks"

Fine for a fitness app, wrong for a food bank. Using a food bank can feel sensitive, so the site should feel calm, private and respectful, never like a game.

Even more animation

The site now has enough movement to feel modern. More would make it slower on cheap phones and tiring for staff who use it all evening. New movement should only help people see what changed.

My suggestion for what to do next: first T1 (watch 5 people), because it may change which ideas matter most. Then the six top picks, one at a time, each tried by you before the next one starts.
